The situation

Concentration is a strategy. Just not a good one.

No. 1

One country. One bank. One point of failure.

Your company, your accounts, your family's savings — all under one flag, one regulator, one banking system. You'd never architect software that way. It's worth asking why your net worth is architected that way.

No. 2

The old way costs $30K and six months.

Traditional firms bill by the hour and think in quarters. You'll receive a beautifully formatted memo, a genuinely impressive volume of invoices, and — somewhere around month five — an entity.

No. 3

Structures don't fail on day one. They fail in year two.

A missed annual report here, a lapsed registered agent there, and a carefully built structure quietly becomes a liability. Setup is a project. Compliance is a practice. Most providers only sell the first one.

Questions

Asked frequently. Answered dryly.

Is this legal?

Yes. Boringly so. Every structure we build is fully reportable in your home jurisdiction — and we prepare the reports. The exciting kind of offshore is the kind that ends in a documentary. We practice the other kind.

Isn't "offshore" a red flag?

The word has a reputation; the practice has a legal framework. Jurisdictional diversification is what every multinational, university endowment, and sensible family office already does. We simply made it available without the mahogany.

How long does setup actually take?

Weeks, not quarters. A single company with a bank account can be live in under a month; a multi-entity structure with a trust takes longer. You'll get a real timeline on the first call, and then — this is the novel part — we'll keep to it.

What does it cost?

Meaningfully less than the law firm quote you're currently avoiding. Setup is a flat fee agreed in advance; maintenance is one annual plan. No six-minute increments, no surprise invoices, and no charge for asking questions — even the ones you preface with "quick question."

Do I need to be ultra-wealthy?

No. If you run a business, hold meaningful savings, or have a family you'd like to keep things orderly for, you're in range. And if you're not there yet, we'll say so on the first call and tell you when to come back. Free advice is our loss leader.

What happens when regulations change?

We adjust the structure, file the paperwork, and send you a short note afterward. Tracking regulatory change across jurisdictions is most of what the maintenance plan is. It is profoundly boring work, which is exactly how compliance should feel.

Who do you turn away?

Anyone who can't pass verification, and anyone whose first question is about secrecy rather than structure. We verify every client and every source of funds. If that sounds tedious — it is, and it's non-negotiable.
